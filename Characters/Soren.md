Soren is [[Elion]]'s brother and rival, engineered as a fetus by [[Egoei]] alongside Elion.


## Ideology
Soren's worldview is shaped mainly by witnessing widespread suffering and how power gets abused between people — personal loss ([[Supporting Cast#Chara|Chara]]'s death, [[Aurelia|Aurelia]]'s fall) is the inciting event, not the core cause. His "highest good" becomes the ending of all suffering - both his own and the suffering of others. As he realizes just how deep suffering, pain, loss, and distinction between peoples goes, he convinces himself that any differences between people ultimately breeds pain. He essentially becomes a radical version of a "post-Marxist" who believes suffering only ends by dissolving all distinctions and disparities between people: cultures, religions, belief systems, and so on. His first concrete move toward this is severing all beings from the [[Lattice]] so nobody but himself retains access to Ki, reasoning that Ki/power is the single biggest source of disparity between people — he intends to address other social and cultural distinctions afterward.

Despite this ideological framing, Soren is read as ultimately sharing Egoei's core selfishness: he believes ending his own suffering (including suffering caused by others' suffering) is worth any cost. This is part of the "Cross vs. Throne" contrast at the heart of the story — Soren seeks the Throne (mechanical, calculated top-down control meant to eliminate suffering, which breeds tyranny instead), while Elion embraces the Cross (stepping into the mud and enduring others' suffering unilaterally).

## Relationship with Elion
Elion and Soren start off with a complex relationship. They're close but because Soren was initially an idealistic man and Elion was someone who had no sympathy for those beneath/weaker than him, they clashed morally and Soren chastized and lectured him often. Soren also felt a sense of superiority to Elion that sometimes slipped out. That said, they are also brothers and close to one another initially despite this. As the story continues in the beginning, the rift grows as their power gap closes and Elion feels as if he's being treated as a child and lesser than Soren despite genuinely trying to help at time. They clash in a climactic battle and then are then separated by circumstances towards the end of arc 3 (circumstances being the destruction of their planet...) They reunite much later in arc 5, but by this time, the two of them have changed significantly: Soren has begun to radicalize after exposure to the wider galaxy and dealing with his own suffering, while Elion has begun to change himself - both for better and, under Egoei's tutelage, worse. By arc 7, Soren and Elion have completely been forced apart by the rift in their ideologies, and Soren's post marxist ideals begin to darken with the colors of Egoei's egoism - on some level, Soren's ideology is the same as Egoei's.

## Awakening (Book 1)
Soren fully awakens in Book 1, in the Maristadiz sanctuary, pinned under Dioikitis's boot with a cracked skull.
- The trigger is pure revulsion at the RHS's cruelty and a wish for the power to stop people like them: *"If I had ANY power to stop people like this… I would-"*. His purple [[Rune Eyes]] manifest, his skull heals almost instantly, and he overwhelms Dioikitis's squad.
- Seeds of his later arc show right away: *"With these eyes, I have the strength to make them pay their dues"*; his flash of *"Who are they to deny him entry? What gives them the right?"* at the capital gates; and Elion's *"…has he always been this brutal?"* (See [[Soren Character Arc]] for how this fits the larger progression.)
- By the end of Book 1 his household knows about his "crazy weird eyes." It's an intentional leak: people know he has wild powers, not what he is.

## Absorption
Soren's [[Rune Eyes]] manifestation lets him absorb and permanently retain any [[Mantras|mantra]] or [[Seals|seal]]-based power used against him (including the effects of seal-bearing mechanisms and relics, like Michani's flamethrower in Book 1) — first demonstrated in Arc 1, when he takes and later reuses [[Supporting Cast#Dioikitis & Michani|Dioikitis]]'s "Simple Mantra: Spearing Thunder." Absorbing requires his eyes to be fully activated. As the eyes fail from exhaustion, he can no longer absorb. Retention is permanent even though mastery isn't: he keeps everything he absorbs, but stays imperfect at wielding it, unlike a mantra properly bound to his own soul.

Thematically this maps to the "Artificial/Man-made Goods vs. Created Goods" motif applied to power itself. Absorption is derivative and parasitic — Soren's strength is built out of what he's taken from others, requiring no personal talent to forge a seal or mantra of his own. This contrasts directly with Elion's adaptation power (see [[Adaptation Framework]]), which is built entirely from his own agonizing effort and physical/mental reshaping, taking nothing from anyone.

By the endgame this gives Soren a uniquely broad, one-of-a-kind arsenal — a necessary counterweight once Elion's own adaptation power has escalated to its late-series ceiling.

## Death
In the final clash in arc 7, Soren refuses reconciliation with Elion and dies trying to draw more power from the [[Aethercrux]] — his soul is consumed by it in the attempt.
