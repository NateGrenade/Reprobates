**Central Theme: The Cross vs. The Throne**
This is the thematic label for the story's central conflict. Soren seeks the Throne: mechanical, calculated top-down control meant to eliminate suffering, which instead breeds tyranny. Elion embraces the Cross: stepping into the mud, absorbing the world's brokenness, and enduring others' suffering unilaterally.

**"The Four Men"**
An early personal organizing concept numbering the main cast by thematic/chronological order: Revenant (1st), Morpheus (2nd), Soren (3rd), Elion (4th).

**Structure**
- Arc 1: The Relic Hunters' Society (led by Dioikitis and Michani) serve as the antagonist faction; Aeros's suffering triggers Elion's Being Called.
- Arc 2: Aurelia, Elion's home planet, is destroyed, expanding the scope of the setting.
- Arcs 3–5: Elion travels with the Companions (an Aurelian survivor and a Galactic companion) and faces the Indifferentist Bounty Hunter and Legion, both serving Revenant. The Companions abandon Elion in Arc 5 when his morality darkens under Morpheus's influence.
- Transition (Act 3 to Act 4): Elion's rage-fueled assault on Revenant's capital destroys an entire city and ends with Revenant near death. Morpheus then personally kills Revenant to obtain what he needs (locating the bio-engineering lab and the Aethercrux) and kills himself on the battlefield to be resurrected — without explaining any of this to Elion. Revenant's imposed peace collapses into faction warfare immediately after.
- Arc 6: A galactic civil war, incited by Elion's own mistakes, which he ultimately fails to stop by the series' end.

**Final Fight**
Morpheus and Soren together — not individually — represent Elion's ultimate test. Morpheus represents the temptation to use the Aethercrux for Elion's own desires; using it to win, or even just to kill Morpheus, would mean Elion enacting Morpheus's own philosophy of eliminating whatever causes him suffering by any justified means. Morpheus cannot conceive of someone willingly sacrificing themselves for people who hate them, and sees the fight as choosing his spiritual successor regardless of who wins. Elion wins by destroying the Aethercrux rather than using it, sacrificing his eyes, divinity, and power. Soren refuses reconciliation and dies trying to draw more power from the Aethercrux instead, his soul consumed by it.

**Ending**
Elion ends the main series at a material, physical, and relational low — having lost nearly everything, including relationships with old companions who reject him — while still holding to his convictions and continuing to serve, expecting nothing in return. No explicit divine reward or sign of cosmic vindication is given (see power_system/awakening.md for the reasoning behind that choice).
