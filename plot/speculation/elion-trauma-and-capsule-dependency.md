# Elion's Trauma, Emotional Suppression, and Capsule Dependency

**Status: SPECULATION**

## Overview

Following the destruction of Aurelia at the end of Arc 2, Elion escapes into space having lost effectively everything: his home, family, friends, Chara, and Soren, whose fate is unknown. He enters a foreign galaxy whose language, culture, and moral framework are fundamentally alien to him.

This speculation proposes that Arcs 3–5 explore not only Elion's grief, but his increasingly unhealthy attempts to avoid confronting it. A recurring element is a drug-like capsule that temporarily suppresses his emotional pain. Elion initially believes he is using it responsibly, but gradually develops a severe dependency while convincing himself that it is helping him recover.

The eventual reveal in late Arc 5 reframes much of Elion's behavior throughout the middle of the story.

## The Foreign Galaxy

After Aurelia's destruction, Elion enters a galaxy whose civilization has largely discarded traditional moral concepts.

Ideas such as good, evil, virtue, duty, restraint, and moral obligation are regarded as antiquated relics. Individuals largely pursue whatever gives them the greatest immediate satisfaction, regardless of whether their actions are destructive, illicit, immoral, or harmful to others.

The point is not necessarily that every individual in the galaxy is malicious. Rather, Elion has entered a civilization in which the moral assumptions underlying his own worldview are no longer culturally shared.

This creates a particularly hostile environment for him psychologically.

Elion has already lost everyone who affirmed his identity and values. Now he is surrounded by people who regard many of those values as obsolete or meaningless. The galaxy therefore continually challenges:

- Elion's understanding of good and evil
- his belief in self-restraint
- his understanding of virtue
- his sense of duty toward others
- his belief that suffering can have meaning
- his conception of what a person ought to do rather than merely what they want to do

Arc 3 can therefore function as an ideological as well as psychological struggle. Elion is grieving Aurelia while simultaneously living in a civilization that provides almost no external reinforcement for the worldview he is trying to preserve.

## Elion's Psychological State

Elion escapes Aurelia physically, but he does not meaningfully recover from its destruction.

His grief compounds with isolation, cultural alienation, and the absence of any clear purpose. At points, this may develop into a much darker contemplation of whether continuing to live is worthwhile.

This does not necessarily need to become an explicit or continuous desire for suicide. A more important underlying condition may be that Elion can no longer identify a future he actually wants.

He is alive, but the world that gave his life meaning is gone.

This creates a distinction between wanting to die and being unable to find a reason to continue living.

## The Capsules

At some point in Arc 3, Elion discovers a strange capsule that, when taken, temporarily alleviates his emotional pain.

The effect should primarily be emotional anesthesia rather than euphoria. The capsule allows him to:

- think about Aurelia without experiencing the full intensity of the pain
- sleep more easily
- function normally
- interact with others
- temporarily regain a sense of stability
- continue living without confronting the full weight of his grief

This makes the capsule extremely easy for Elion to rationalize.

He tells himself that he will use it sparingly and that it is helping him. From his immediate perspective, this is even partially true: taking the capsule makes his life more bearable.

The deeper problem is that the capsule prevents him from properly experiencing and processing the emotions he needs to confront.

Elion is not healing. He is repeatedly anesthetizing himself.

## Gradual Dependency

The progression should initially be subtle.

Elion does not consciously decide to become dependent on the capsules. Instead, he repeatedly discovers that taking one makes difficult emotions easier to tolerate.

Early use might appear innocuous:

- Elion takes one before sleeping.
- He takes one after remembering Aurelia.
- He takes one when his grief interferes with his work.
- Someone notices him taking one and he dismisses the concern.
- He becomes defensive when someone suggests he may be using them too frequently.

The audience should initially have little reason to interpret these moments as evidence of severe dependency.

Elion himself should sincerely believe that he has the situation under control.

Over time, however, the capsules become increasingly integrated into his daily life.

## Suppression Rather Than Recovery

The capsule dependency is intended to reinforce a larger flaw in Elion's character: his tendency to seek external solutions to internal suffering.

Earlier in his life, Elion repeatedly attempts to solve problems through action and power. After Aurelia, he encounters a problem that cannot simply be defeated.

The capsule represents the smallest and most desperate version of this impulse:

**He cannot fix his pain, so he tries to make the pain stop.**

This also parallels the larger trajectory of his character. Elion increasingly seeks ways to transform his suffering into something actionable rather than simply enduring and processing it.

The capsules allow him to avoid that process entirely.

## Connection to Egoei

By the time Egoei's influence becomes significant, Elion may have spent years avoiding the grief caused by Aurelia.

This makes his eventual moral decline more psychologically significant.

Egoei does not simply introduce anger into an otherwise healthy Elion. He provides an ideological outlet for pain that Elion has never properly processed.

Elion's suppressed grief, resentment, isolation, and hatred can increasingly be redirected toward external targets.

The emotional anesthesia of the capsules may therefore contribute indirectly to his eventual rage: Elion has never learned how to metabolize his suffering, so when that suffering finally finds an outlet, it can emerge in an extreme form.

This should remain a character interpretation rather than an explicit causal statement unless later canon establishes otherwise.

## The Arc 5 Reveal

The most significant reveal occurs late in Arc 5, after Elion's moral decline causes his companions to abandon him.

Only after he is completely alone does the audience receive a genuine look at his living situation.

His room is in severe disarray.

Hundreds or potentially thousands of empty capsules are scattered throughout the living area. Some are in obvious trash containers, while others have accumulated beneath furniture, around his bed, on surfaces, and among his belongings.

The room itself reflects prolonged neglect:

- clothes and belongings are scattered around
- food or dishes may be left unattended
- ordinary maintenance has been neglected
- the space feels increasingly disconnected from normal habitation
- the capsules have accumulated almost everywhere

The sheer quantity reveals that Elion's use was vastly greater than the audience had previously understood.

The audience realizes that the occasional capsules shown throughout Arcs 3–5 were only the visible fraction of a much larger habit.

## Recontextualization

The reveal should not require an explicit explanation from Elion or another character.

Instead, the audience is left to reconstruct what has been happening.

Scenes from previous arcs can suddenly take on different meanings:

- Elion taking a capsule after an emotionally difficult event
- his insistence that he is fine
- his unusual emotional detachment
- his ability to continue functioning despite catastrophic losses
- his defensiveness about the capsules
- his gradual isolation
- his increasingly poor living conditions

The audience realizes that Elion has been deteriorating for years while maintaining the appearance of functionality.

The capsule did not make him happy.

It allowed him to remain operational.

## Thematic Relationship to the Rune Eyes

The capsules can also function as a dark thematic parallel to Elion's Rune Eyes.

The Rune Eyes fulfill desires through power. The capsules fulfill a much smaller but increasingly desperate desire:

**Make the pain stop.**

Elion's earlier desires tend to involve changing the external world to resolve internal suffering. The capsules represent the point at which he no longer believes he can change the world and instead tries to suppress his own response to it.

This makes the capsules a particularly bleak counterpart to his powers.

His supernatural abilities allow him to transform suffering into strength.

The capsules allow him to temporarily stop feeling the suffering at all.

## Relationship to the Larger Arc

This speculation provides a possible psychological bridge between the end of Arc 2 and Elion's eventual state in Arc 5.

**Arc 2:**  
Elion loses everything.

**Arc 3:**  
Elion attempts to survive in an alien civilization while grieving Aurelia and struggling to preserve his worldview.

**Arcs 3–5:**  
Elion increasingly suppresses his pain through the capsules while convincing himself that he is functioning and recovering.

**Arc 5:**  
His moral decline under Egoei's influence causes his companions to abandon him.

**The reveal:**  
Elion's living situation exposes the extent of the deterioration that had been occurring beneath his outward functionality.

This makes his later collapse feel less like a sudden transformation and more like the culmination of a psychological process that has been occurring for years.

## Status and Cautions

This is currently **SPECULATION**, not established canon.

The exact nature of the capsules, their pharmacological effects, whether they are chemically addictive, and the precise degree to which they contribute to Elion's moral decline remain undecided.

Likewise, the degree to which suicide enters Elion's thoughts should remain flexible until the tone and structure of Arcs 3–5 are further developed.

The core speculative concept is the combination of:

1. Aurelia's destruction leaving Elion psychologically devastated.
2. An alien galaxy fundamentally challenging his moral worldview.
3. Elion becoming increasingly unable to process his grief.
4. The discovery of a substance that temporarily suppresses his emotional pain.
5. Elion convincing himself that controlled use is helping him.
6. Gradual, largely hidden dependency.
7. A late Arc 5 reveal showing the enormous scale of the habit.
8. The dependency serving as a manifestation of Elion's broader refusal or inability to confront his suffering.
