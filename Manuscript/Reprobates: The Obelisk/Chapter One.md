<p style="text-align:center"><strong>Chapter One — </strong></p>

"Give me some good news for once"

"...I'm sorry, my lord"

"’I'm sorry’ isn't good enough anymore, you piece of shit. That's my sister in there! How long have you had to help her?"

"My lord there's nothing that can be done. We can ease the symptoms, but we’ve never seen-"

"HOW... long?"

"A y-"

"A year. That's right. Go in there and look at her! Does that look like a year's worth of care right there, 'doctor'?"

"..."

"ANSWER ME"

"I'm so sorry, my lord."

Elion pauses, glaring down at the quaking man. "Tell everyone to get out of that room now."

The physician doesn't need to be told twice. He scurries in the door, and a few moments of muffled conversation later, infirmarians and specialists come out one by one. Elion watches as they leave, their heads hung low. Once alone, he takes several deep breaths, closes his eyes, and then pushes open the door to the infirmary.

Elion steps inside, and with a low voice, he asks “Chara? Are you awake?” His question is answered immediately when the little girl jumps out of the bed and runs over to him.

“Elion! Why’re you here?” She stops a few feet from him, tuckered out. He puts out his hands and grabs Chara as she sways.

“Easy easy easy. Don’t run.” He picks her up in his arms. *She’s lighter than I remember.* With her in his arms, and much to her chagrin, he walks her back to her bed. “I had an early night: things haven’t been very eventful in the city. So, I figured I’d come visit you before you were asleep.”

Despite her lethargy, she is restless nonetheless. “Well that’s cool. Wait, does this mean you can take me relic hunting again?”

Elion sits at the end of Chara’s bed, staring off at the wall. “N- no. Not yet.” Her face falls just slightly, and very quickly he continues “-but soon. Very soon, in fact. I know you’ve been cooped up for a while, but the doctors are working very hard. You’ll be better in no time, and the minute you are, we’re off to any city or country you choose. Ok?” Elion watches Chara’s smile return in the corner of his eye. Though he can somehow tell that there’s a twinge of doubt in it. “And hey, once you’re up, maybe I can whip you into shape, make you real buff, huh? Sounds fun?” Elion nudges Chara gently “My name is Chara! I have big muscles.” Elion imitates with an overly deep voice, making Chara giggle.

Chara pauses for a very brief moment, deep in thought. Her eyes widen with an idea of unbridled genius. “Oh! Could I get a super massive seal, too? One that shoots lasers! And those lasers shoot more lasers!” Elion laughs in return. “Wow, that’s rather creative. But seals are so cheap. A real fighter doesn’t need outside tools like that. Still… just for you, I could make an exception. But only because it’s such a good idea. Might be a pain to find a weaver who could pull that off, though.” The two of them laugh together, and then Chara goes dead silent, save for a sharp noise in her throat. She goes pale and stops breathing. “Chara? Chara! Chara you’re ok, just breathe. Breathe!” And so she does, but the deafening quiet lasted too long. Far too long. Her breaths are rapid, and they take a while to slow to a normal pace. “You need rest. I’m sorry for riling you up.” Elion’s voice is reassuring, but the way he is subtly trembling betrays a much more unsettled part of him.

Just after the two of them settle in, Elion hears it. “Elion! What are you thinking?” Elion stands up off the bed, his demeanor hardening on a dime. His brother, Soren, walks into the room. “Kicking all of the staff out!? In-” Soren lowers his voice. He smiles softly, a little sadly, at Chara before mouthing “I’ll talk to you in the morning” to her, eliciting a tired, still nerve wracked nod. Soren gestures the infirmarians back into the room, and they shuffle in one after another without making eye contact with Elion. Soren approaches Elion, puts a firm hand on his shoulder, and in a hushed voice whispers “In her condition, she cannot leave these doctors’ sight, you hear me? I understand your frustration, but you need to rein it in.”

Elion opens his mouth to tell Soren to shove it, that Soren’s overstepped his boundaries by overruling his, that-. But Elion glances up at the nurses, who are feeding Chara elixirs and remedies alike. He sees Chara’s small hands shakily hold the medicine cups to her lips. And then he closes his mouth, and a beat later he musters a half-hearted “I understand.” Elion shakes off Soren’s arm and leaves the infirmary. Soren follows. “I’m retiring for the night,” Elion says to Soren, his tone cold.

“Wait. Before you do, let’s take a little walk” Soren interjects. Elion stops with his foot on the first step. “At this hour?”

“Come on. Just for a few. I can tell you need to get your mind off things; going up to be alone won’t help.”

A long sigh follows from Elion, who then shrugs. “Yeah, you’re probably right.”

The night air is crisp. The city is bathed in dancing shadows cast by the streetlamps that line the roads, each one a testament to Ahafurt’s great wealth. While the weather is harsher than he’d prefer, the cold helps Elion divert his focus away from the beggars whose incessant voices call after them. “I can’t understand why we spend the money to light up this part of the city, of all places.”

Soren stops walking, digging in his pockets. “It’s the least we could do for them, don’t you think? The people of this city, even here: they’re the reason we are able to live so lavishly. They’re the reason that Chara can receive…” Soren’s voice peters out as he realizes that now’s not the time to get into that. He gives a few coins from his person to the few destitute bold enough to approach him, who then promptly retreat into the darkness after giving their thanks.

“Of course I know that.” Elion outstretches his hands towards the towering, insurmountable wall that looms at the edge of the city’s skyline. “And we’re the reason that barbarians don’t kill or subjugate every last one of them. We’ve paid our end of the bargain with that alone.” Elion puts his fingers up to his lips, quieting the both of them. In the distance, the indistinct sounds of people struggling can be heard. Someone far off can be heard demanding money. Another one, closer this time, seems to be yelling threats at what sounds like a crying child. “And perhaps if we spent less money on such vain pursuits, we would have more guards to put a stop to **that**, brother. Or we could expand our trade more. Or perhaps we could actually find a useful doctor in this uneducated country of ours.”

Soren’s face sours at the unspeakable yet untraceable sounds. “You’re enjoying this? Using active crime to try and win a late-night argument?” A noise, far more distinct this time, is heard. Clothing tears somewhere down the street, and both Elion and Soren’s heads whip around to pinpoint the origin of the noise.

“Don’t twist my words. There’s nothing more that I hate.” Soren is ready to move, but in a rare occasion between the twins, Elion reacts quicker. They launch from a stop, cracking the cobblestone ground beneath their feet. The two dash down the road at a blinding speed, whizzing past buildings kicking up dust in their wake. Then Elion sees it: a man, perhaps forty, atop a struggling and sobbing woman, clearly malnourished and no more than seventeen.

Soren yells out at the man. “Unhand her! Stop immediately or I-”

The man looks up, but isn’t given a chance to register a thing before Elion is gripping him by the neck. Elion stares into the criminal’s soul. “Give me one good reason.” The man wheezes, only able to draw the most restrictive of breaths. Soren kneels down to the woman on the ground, checking her injuries. The commotion draws people out of their houses: some holding simple knives or pitchforks, a sparse few brandish seals on their hands, others still simply choose to stay indoors and pray they’ll be safe. Elion notices the onlookers, and chooses to calm himself. In fact, he even smirks just a little bit, only visible to the piece of trash gripped in his right hand. Elion murmurs “I guess it’s your lucky day” and opts to throw the man into the air. The lowlife lands in the middle of the street, in the center of the people who’ve come out to watch. They gasp and back away. Elion looks back at Soren, still smirking. “This is the way I pay off the debt to my citizens, Soren.” He walks to the incapacitated man and pins him to the ground beneath his foot. “Filth like you ruin Ahafurt for its many law-abiding, virtuous citizens. You’re disgraceful. But your arrest will symbolize our people’s dedication to moral virtue.” One person begins to tentatively clap. Then another. Some say “Thank you, my lords!” Others begin smiling.

Soren watches Elion bask in the applause of the crowd, but focuses his attention on the injured woman in front of him. Her crying is drowned out by the praise of the crowd. Very few even bother to bat an eye toward her. *If they’re not claiming the glory, they’re ignoring everyone else. This noble system that treats us like gods.*

*How I hate it.*

Minutes pass and the commotion dies down. The scoundrel of a man is handed off to guards to be imprisoned. The woman is found by her parents and bandaged up. Then, only Elion and Soren remain in the now desolate area. Elion, still sporting a satisfied grin, mutters “he’ll be pleased to hear about this.”

Soren shakes his head. “Don’t play their game, Elion.”

“What game? Just because I enjoy being thanked doesn’t make this a game to me”

“That’s not what I meant. How long will you let our parents drive you to this behavior?”

“...Don’t moralize to me, Soren. Not tonight. I should’ve checked on the woman first, I get it. I’ve had enough fighting for one night, haven’t you? We should start heading back, I have things to attend to.”

Soren narrows his eyes just a hair when Elion tries to change the subject, but he heeds Elion’s plea for a ceasefire. “Alright, let’s go. But I know you don’t do that, back there, for the ‘citizens’. You might as well not lie to them.” Elion side-eyes Soren but gives no response. Rather, he has none that he’s willing to voice.

They return to the manor. They ascend the stairs together and each head off to their bedrooms.

“Goodnight, Elion.”

“Goodnight, Soren.”

Elion opens his door and pauses, waiting until he hears his brother’s door creak shut. Elion’s face seems relaxed, and his features melt into something more exhausted. He shuts his own door without going inside of his room, and waits in place until he hears Soren’s footsteps stop.

Elion moves through the now-empty halls towards his personal study. He opens the door and latches it shut behind him. He lights an oil lamp on his desk, takes a seat in a wooden chair, and begins to read. Parchment notes are piled across the desk, scattered on the floor, and tucked inside of leather-covered books. Ink stains have sunk deep into the porous desk’s wood, and the same is true for the floor. There are a humble few bookshelves in the study, all of which appear to have been turned nearly inside out. Elion’s lost track of how many nights he’s spent poring through every ounce of literature he can get his hands on. Books on seals, Ki manipulation, modern mechanisms, anatomy, medicine, remedies, ancient arts, and now mythology: each genre representing different sleepless weeks spent searching for some way, any way, to help Chara, or at least to ease her symptoms. Nothing.

“Born sickly as she was, it’s a miracle she’s lived as long as she has in the first place.” Elion would be lying if he said those words hadn’t appeared in his head more than once this past year, and they’ve been growing more common with each passing month. Every time they appear, he forces it down harder. *I have* *wealth and unlimited strength: there’s nothing in this world that is out of my reach. I just need to find it. *That exact phrase is the one he’d say to himself ad nauseam until the morbid nagging of his deeper thoughts subsided. All the while, he’d write and write, read and read, painstakingly go through each letter of a book to find even a single instance of hope.

Tonight is no different. He has already gone through three of seven volumes of *Relics in Antiquity*, and as expected, there isn’t much relevant information for Chara. Despite posturing itself as a scholarly collection, the author seems much more interested in dramatizing the legends or folk tales surrounding ancient relics, rather than analyzing their precise retellings. With each night he reads, Elion finds that some relics, frankly, shouldn’t even be in a series as acclaimed as the one he’s reading.

Regardless, Elion forces himself to continue researching. He spends an hour reading through a particularly lengthy and nonsensical chapter about The Aethercrux. *This series grows more fantastical with each page. All powerful? A gift forged by God Himself? What sort of- *Elion stands up, enraged at having wasted so much precious time on yet another fairy tale.

“WORTHLESS GARBAGE!”

He throws the book aside, where it hits into the ground with an unceremonious thwap. He stands there in the study, burnt out and no closer to a cure than last month, or last summer, or last year. He sits back down at his chair, and his eyes return to the book still laying on the floor. With no other options, Elion picks it back up and, reluctantly, begins reading the next chapter.

The sun hardly seems to stay down anymore. Every night Elion spends sifting through literature, the dawn arrives in what feels like minutes, just to taunt him. Closing Volume III of *Relics in Antiquity*, Elion stands up and stretches just as the birds begin to sing their morning songs.

Elion strolls down the steps of the manor, doing his level best to appear awake and alert. As he walks, he begins to catalogue his obligations for the day to mentally prepare himself. *Reports likely need handling. Mother and Father will take care of finances and delegation, as per usual. Physical training this afternoon. Then of course… *The list seems unendingly large, but such is the case for him ever since he’s begun squandering his evening and nighttime hours to private studying, rather than taking care of civil or personal matters. Lamentably, Elion hasn’t been able to return to his hobby of relic hunting for months because of it. Though, increasing responsibility for Ahafurt has steadily depleted his available free time compared to years prior. *And yet they still haven’t let us inherit the city which we practically govern. *The bitter thought crosses Elion’s mind, but it’s quickly expunged after his focus returns to his immediate concerns.

Elion sits down to eat all alone. His parent’s absence was expected, but Soren is more punctual than he, and Elion is already minutes late. The manor as a whole is strangely quiet. He tries to ignore it, but even sleep deprived as he is, he’s unable to drown out the heavy silence, whose only disturbance is the faintest shuffling of workers somewhere deep in the halls. Elion finishes his food and sits in the dining room, as if waiting for someone to arrive and disperse the fog of quiet that has settled in the estate.

Elion rises from the table and navigates to Soren’s room, only to find it vacant. Deeply confused, he intrudes into his parents’ room, which is also empty. He walks through the hallways until, for the first time in forever, he spots someone walking at the end of a corridor.

“You. Where is my brother?”

The young man jumps at the sudden address. “Lord Soren? I apologize, but I haven’t seen him since last night.”

Elion dismisses the kid with a grumble. *I might as well visit her. Perhaps Soren is there as well. After all, he didn’t get the time last night. *He descends the flight of stairs and paces towards the infirmary. More workers seem concentrated in the area than normal: far more, in fact. Several are waiting right at the doorway, where both Soren and their parents are standing as well. His brother’s eyes are wide, his mother’s face is a mixture of shock and something akin to sorrow, and his father’s face is unmoved. The lead physician stands among them, and they are engaged in intense conversation. Elion pushes his way through the gathering crowd of nurses and care workers.

“What’s going on here? What’s happening?”

Soren is too distracted to even look at him, while the lead physician notes his arrival with a single, sympathetic glance before he delves back into his explanation. The words that hit his ears make Elion’s heart beat so hard in his chest that it muffles the conversation around him.

“The tissue damage on her… has been unable to heal, even with constant aid… with her constitution… growing more chronically ill… her heart and lungs in particular… She’s become extremely fragile… We don’t see improvement as the goal anymore, and even keeping her stable is failing… treatments that once worked… we are beginning end of…”

The man seems to go on forever, carefully weaving technical jargon and academic detachment to conceal his failures.

“We estimate she has weeks left, not months… Likely not even **a** month.”

Elion looks around with a dazed expression, searching desperately for someone to expose the lie for what it was. Anyone who can point out the flaw in the doctor’s logic. Just one person who has their doubts about the drawn conclusion. But not a soul pipes up. “You’re wrong…”

Their father cuts in with a stern, dark voice. “Elion.”

The nurses and workers around him look over with commiserating expressions. The doctor returns to his familiar line: “I’m so sorry, my L-”

“I’m telling you: you’re wrong. This time next month, Chara will be on her feet, adventuring around the world.”

“Enough! Don’t make idiotic promises unless you can keep them.” Elion’s father erupts in anger, brandishing the same unimpressed, unconvinced, and impatient expression he has always given him.

“I will keep it. You hear me? All of you!” Elion points at the workers, the servants gathered around, and then at his father. “I swear on my pride as a nobleman, on my pride as a **man**, Chara will leave that room on her own two feet! Tell the world that the youngest noble of Ahafurt is less than a month from being cured!”

Elion is met with silence. The doctor has a pitying expression. The nurses and infirmarians are in silent shock. His mother won’t make eye contact with him. And Soren, especially, appears horrified and enraged all at once. However, Elion’s father refuses to break eye contact.

“Ok son, I believe you. Why don’t you begin, then? There’s no time to waste.”

Elion flinches, though unnoticeably, at the dismissal. “I would like to first visit Chara”

“You’ll have plenty of time to do so, if you really intend on fulfilling your word.” This time, the tone of Elion’s father’s voice suggests there’s no more conversation to be had.
