Egoei is a philosophically articulate antagonist and, in an engineered sense, the father/creator of both Elion and Soren.

**Backstory**
Egoei and Aioni were once close, something like best friends, before Aioni's purge of the Atheri — a purge Aioni carried out because he believed Atheri immortality bred cruelty. Aioni personally left Egoei blinded and mortally wounded during that purge, though Egoei survived and escaped. After escaping, Egoei fell in love and started a family on a distant planet. Aioni's bounty hunters eventually found and killed that family, branding them traitors, despite Egoei begging them to spare his family and take him instead. This is what cemented his shift toward believing mortal compassion is worthless and mortal lives don't merit consideration.

**Ideology**
Egoei's rhetoric draws on ethical egoism and immortal-scale consequentialism. He lives in hiding, spreading his philosophy onto Elion as a chosen successor. He's ironically "selfless" only in the narrow sense that he values his people's continued existence even above himself — unlike Soren, whose selflessness is read as ultimately self-serving.

**Plan and Death**
Egoei's true goal is locating a machine that creates new Atheri bodies, plus a soul-immortality artifact called the Aethercrux — both hidden by Aioni. He doesn't die and resurrect the way a typical shonen villain would; he deliberately transfers his soul into an already-prepared new body the instant he remotely connects to his machine. In the climax, after Elion's rage-fueled assault leaves Aioni near death, Egoei steps in, uses the dying Aioni to locate the bio-engineering lab and the Aethercrux, personally kills Aioni, and then kills himself on the battlefield — having gotten what he needs to be "reprinted." He doesn't bother explaining any of this to Elion, underscoring how little Egoei actually cares about him or anyone but himself. Aioni's imposed peace collapses into faction warfare immediately afterward, marking the story's transition from Act 3 to Act 4.

Egoei's soul is bound to the Aethercrux via an ancient deal for soul-immortality, struck after a confrontation with Aioni millions of years ago — meaning destroying the Aethercrux later in the story kills him for good.

**Thematic Role in the Final Fight**
Egoei and Soren together — not individually — represent Elion's ultimate test. Egoei represents the temptation to use the Aethercrux for Elion's own desires (such as resurrecting Chara); if Elion uses it to win, or even just to kill Egoei, Egoei still wins ideologically, since Elion would be enacting Egoei's own philosophy of eliminating whatever causes him suffering by any justified means. Egoei cannot conceive of someone willingly sacrificing themselves for people who hate them, and sees the fight as choosing his spiritual successor regardless of who actually wins.
