# Soren — Character Arc

**Status: Provisional**

## Core Arc

Soren's radicalization begins with a collapse rather than an awakening. He initially tries to cling to his old beliefs and convictions while confronting a universe that appears profoundly cruel and indifferent to suffering. He continues trying to live according to those convictions even as reality repeatedly undermines his faith in their ability to accomplish anything meaningful.

Eventually, the strain becomes unbearable. Soren falls into complete and total despair. He comes to believe that his efforts are worthless: that trying to do good, uphold his convictions, or resist the cruelty of the world accomplishes nothing in the face of the scale of suffering around him.

Only after reaching this point does Soren realize something that radically changes the direction of his life: **he actually has the power to change things.**

This realization becomes the foundation of his radicalization. Soren begins to reason that if he possesses the ability to meaningfully alter the galaxy and reduce its suffering, then he has not merely the ability but the **right—and eventually the obligation—to do so.** The moral question shifts from *"What should I believe?"* to *"Why should I permit this world to remain as it is when I have the power to change it?"*

His despair therefore does not simply make him nihilistic. It destroys his previous moral framework and leaves a vacuum that is filled by an increasingly absolute conviction in his own ability to impose a better order upon reality.

## Psychological Progression

**Conviction → Suffering → Despair → Power → Radicalization**

Soren's radicalization should feel less like a gradual descent into misery and more like a catastrophic transformation of his response to misery. He spends a substantial portion of the story deeply depressed, convinced that nothing he does matters. His eventual fanaticism can therefore initially resemble a recovery.

As Soren embraces his new ideology, he may become more energetic, passionate, purposeful, and outwardly alive than he was during his depression. He has finally found an answer to the helplessness that destroyed him. Unfortunately, the answer is control.

He no longer has to wait for the universe to become better. He believes he can **make it** better.

## Central Tragedy

Soren does not necessarily become evil because he stops caring about people. He becomes evil because his desire to relieve suffering becomes detached from the limits imposed by morality, human dignity, freedom, and legitimate distinctions between persons and societies.

His reasoning can ultimately become:

> If I can save people, then refusing to do so is itself immoral.

From there, increasingly horrifying conclusions become possible. Individual freedom, cultural identity, religion, hierarchy, personal choice, and other distinctions can all become obstacles if Soren believes they permit suffering to continue.

The tragedy is that Soren's fanaticism gives him something his old worldview could not: certainty that his actions can accomplish something. He escapes despair not by discovering that the universe is good, but by deciding that **he will become the force that makes it good.**

## Relationship to Elion

Soren's arc should serve as a thematic counterpoint to Elion's development.

Elion's first arc teaches him that he is wrong, without yet giving him a complete answer for how to be right. Soren's trajectory can instead revolve around the discovery that he is not powerless—and the catastrophic mistake of treating that power as a moral authorization to reshape the world according to his own judgment.

Where Elion must learn humility after believing himself capable of heroism, Soren can become increasingly convinced that his capacity to act gives him the authority to decide what is best for everyone else.

## Status / Caveat

This character arc is **Provisional**. The broad psychological progression and thematic endpoint are established as a working concept, but the specific events, timing, ideology, and stages of Soren's radicalization remain open to revision as the larger story develops.
